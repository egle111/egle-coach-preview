(03) How i got here

The promotion is the easy part.

I spent my career in tech as a UX designer, product manager, head of design and startup founder. I've been an IC, a manager and a people leader.

And I've watched the same thing happen again and again. A business promotes its best IC to lead people: new title, pay rise, "good luck." Nobody explains what good leadership looks like.

Leading people came naturally to me, and I still hit moments no training had prepared me for. Rude pushback. Someone not meeting expectations. Pressure from above.

When new leaders don't get support, hard conversations get avoided and the bar slips. Decisions climb to senior leaders, strong people disengage and problems surface late.

So I built the programme I wish existed. Leaders work on real situations from their own team, personalised to their role.

If you're promoting people into leadership, let's talk.

(05) How we work

How this looks in practice

01

Learn it alongside people in the same seat.

Each module takes on a challenge every people leader hits: stepping into the role, setting expectations, holding the standard, influencing upward, building team ownership and making it stick.

Sessions mix short, practical teaching with discussion and rehearsal. Leaders bring a real situationd, discuss with the group, and practise what they'll actually say.

E.g. someone on your team is missing the mark and you've been putting off the conversation. In Hold the standard, you plan it, practise it, and leave knowing what you'll say and what happens next.

02

Real examples, not right answers.

After each module, leaders complete a piece of structured written work. The first is about themselves: the values they lead by, the strengths that got them promoted, and the habits that could get in the way when they're under pressure. Later work turns to the team: expectations, decision ownership, a difficult conversation.

There are no ideal answers. Leaders write about real moments from their own work, not what a good manager is supposed to say. They bring it to their coaching session, so the conversation starts from something specific.

E.g. after the first module, each leader names a strength that helped them succeed as an individual contributor and writes about a recent moment where it didn't work with their team.

03

Explore and rehearse

Each leader has six individual sessions with me. They bring their homework and whatever is hardest right now, and we start from there.

I'll question the assumptions and habits behind how they're leading, say what I see, and help them work out what to do about it. Then we rehearse the conversations and decisions they need to own, so they're not trying them for the first time with their team.

Every session ends with one practical next step to take before we meet again.

E.g. a leader brings the feedback conversation they've prepared. We look at what's making it hard to start, practise the opening, and rehearse how they'll handle pushback.

04

Use it at work

Between sessions, leaders take the next step they agreed in coaching. They have the conversation, clarify an expectation or make the decision they’ve been putting off.

They bring what happened back to the next module or coaching session: what they tried, how people responded and what still needs work. We use that to decide what comes next.

E.g. a leader has the feedback conversation they rehearsed, agrees what needs to change and follows up. In their next session, we look at how it went and what they need to do next.

(06) Before we talk

Who is the programme for?

Fast-growing companies where people are moving into management quickly. First-time managers, ICs about to step up, and experienced managers who want to lead better.

Is it suitable for managers who already have some leadership experience?

Yes. The programme is tailored to their experience and the leadership challenges they’re facing now.

How much time will each leader need to commit?

Nine hours of module sessions and six 1:1 coaching sessions per leader, plus alignment, reviews and applying the learning at work.

Are sessions online or in person?

Both module sessions and 1:1 coaching can take place online or in person.

Can the programme be tailored to our organisation?

Always. The programme is tailored to your organisation and each leader’s role, team and development needs. Modules can also vary accordingly.

How are managers and sponsors involved?

The leader's manager is involved from the start. Before the programme begins, they join a 45-minute alignment session to agree responsibilities, decision authority and expectations. As sponsor, they then review progress with me three times, against goals set at the start, at 60, 120 and 180 days (30, 60 and 90 in the intensive). That's about three hours of their time in total.

What stays confidential in the 1:1 coaching sessions?

What's said in 1:1 sessions stays between the leader and me. The company sees progress against agreed goals, not what we discussed. I only share more if the leader agrees, the law requires it, or there's a serious and immediate risk of harm. Where possible, I'll talk to them first.

How will we know the programme is making a difference?

We agree what progress looks like at the start, then the sponsor and I review it three times. We look for visible changes in how the leader leads and how the team works, not whether they finished the programme.
